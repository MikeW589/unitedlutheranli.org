Our Legacy & Heritage

Three distinct pathways of grace, service, and community on Long Island, unified in mission and future.

St. Peter's Evangelical Lutheran Church

Serving railroad pioneers, families, and builders in Huntington Station for over a century.

The Railroad Suburb & "Fairground"

St. Peter's began doing ministry in 1908 in the area then known as "Fairground" due to local racetracks and county fairs. When the Long Island Rail Road (LIRR) established the "Huntington Station" two miles south of the main village, St. Peter's emerged to serve the rapidly expanding railroad suburb. The congregation initially rented space in a small Presbyterian chapel on New York Avenue.

The "Little White Church"

Outgrowing its rented space, the founding members purchased land on Fairgrounds Avenue and dedicated a classic wooden church building in 1912. In 1924, an addition was constructed to keep pace with the growing Sunday School. In the late 1950s, this beautiful white structure was sold to the local Greek Orthodox community, becoming the founding home of St. Paraskevi Church.

Ogden Court Expansion & Modern Sanctuary

After World War II, St. Peter's bought land on Ogden Court, building its Christian Education Building by 1955. In 1969, St. Peter's completed its modern connecting sanctuary, complete with a landmark bell tower, an exceptional pipe organ, and a magnificent stained-glass window depicting Christ as "The Light of the World".

Theological Inclusivity

Committed to theological progress, St. Peter's joined the Association of Evangelical Lutheran Churches (AELC) in 1977, paving the way for the creation of the inclusive Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA) to advance gender equity, ordain women, and foster ecumenical partnerships.

Legacy Profile

Archival Landmark 1969 modern sanctuary, bell tower, and "Light of the World" stained-glass window.
Core Legacy Strength Suburban railroad ministry, education growth, and Chancel/Bell choirs.

Gloria Dei Lutheran Church

Deep Swedish roots, family fellowship, and a legacy of compassion in Huntington Station.

Augustana Synod Roots

Founded in 1931, Gloria Dei began as a mission congregation of the historic Augustana Synod, a primarily Swedish branch of American Lutheranism. Its Latin name, meaning "the glory of God", was chosen to stand as a constant sign of God's presence and grace in South Huntington.

Generations of Care & Louise Palmieri

For over nine decades, generations of Huntington Station families built their faith and friendships at the 18th Street campus. Members like Louise Palmieri—who taught Sunday School, ran the nursery program, and led the handicraft guild for over 60 years—infused the campus with a warm, family-centered atmosphere of care.

Transitioning into a Mercy & Outreach Center

In 2022, Gloria Dei merged with St. Peter's and King of Kings, moving its Sunday services to the St. Peter's campus. This allowed the 18th Street facility to expand its vital community care work. Today, it hosts Joseph's Storehouse food pantry and Joseph's Closet, serving over 15,000 to 26,000 individuals annually, alongside weekly recovery groups and Boy Scouts.

Legacy Profile

Founded 1931
Archival Landmark 1930s Chapel and Sanctuary on 18th Street.
Core Legacy Strength Augustana Swedish heritage, handicraft guild, and client-choice storehouse mercy.

King of Kings Lutheran Church

Melville's neighborhood community anchor, ecumenical pioneer, and civic sanctuary.

Melville Community Sanctuary

Established as a neighborhood community church, King of Kings has long served as a critical inter-faith, civic, and recovery partner in the Melville area. Its cozy facility on New York Avenue has been a welcoming presence for local residents, support groups, and regional partnerships.

Ecumenical Jewish Partnership

In early 1969, King of Kings played a historic role in Long Island Jewish history by hosting the very first organizational and worship meetings for Temple Beth Torah (now a prominent Reform Jewish congregation) while they were building their synagogue. To honor this relationship, the two congregations held joint, rotating Thanksgiving Eve services for decades.

The Golf Match Wager & Melville AA

In 1982, the Melville Group (AA) Saturday night meeting was founded at King of Kings. The meeting arose from a legendary friendly golf match wager between church member Tony Z. and the pastor. Over time, the meeting grew into a key Melville recovery center, eventually adding Al-Anon and beginner study groups that continue to meet weekly.

Multicultural & Civic Legacy

Today, the campus continues its civic and multicultural legacy under the United Lutheran Church, hosting the Sung Mood Korean Methodist Church and Mission Vida Church, hosting Melville AAA/Al-Anon groups, and serving as a vital polling place for the Suffolk County Board of Elections.

Legacy Profile

Founded 1960s
Archival Landmark Melville Sanctuary and Parsonage.
Core Legacy Strength Civic nesting partner, ecumenical interfaith support, and recovery group sponsor.
