HONORING OUR INDIVIDUAL ROOTS

Our Three Campus Legacies

Each campus preserves a century of local history and a deep commitment to serving the Huntington and Melville communities. Click any campus to explore its full historical story.

Established 1908

St. Peter's Campus Legacy

11 Ogden Court, Huntington Station, NY 11746

The Railroad Suburb & "Fairground"

St. Peter's began doing ministry in Huntington Station in 1908, back when the surrounding town was known as "Fairground" due to local racetracks and county fairs. When the Long Island Rail Road (LIRR) was denied permission to lay tracks through Huntington Village, they built the station two miles south, birthing the "Huntington Station" suburb. St. Peter's emerged to serve this new community, initially renting space in a small Presbyterian chapel on New York Avenue.

From the "Little White Church" to Ogden Court

Having outgrown its rented space, the founding membership dedicated its first classic white wooden church building on Fairgrounds Avenue in 1912, adding an addition in 1924. After World War II, the church was bursting at the seams. In 1951, they purchased another plot farther south on Ogden Court, dedicating the Christian Education Building in 1955. (The original white building was sold to the Greek Orthodox community, becoming the founding sanctuary for St. Paraskevi).

Stained Glass, Organ, & Inclusive Faith

In 1969, St. Peter's dedicated its large modern sanctuary with a soaring bell tower, a historic pipe organ, and a magnificent stained-glass window depicting Christ as *"The Light of the World"*. Committed to progress, in 1977 the parish joined the Association of Evangelical Lutheran Churches (AELC) and later the inclusive ELCA, supporting gender equality and community cooperation.

Established 1931

Gloria Dei Campus Legacy

22 East 18th Street, Huntington Station, NY 11746

The Augustana Lutheran Heritage

Gloria Dei was founded in 1931 as a mission congregation of the Augustana Synod, a primarily Swedish branch of American Lutheranism. Its beautiful Latin name translates to "the glory of God," serving as a constant testament to God’s wonderful presence in Huntington Station.

Sixty Years of Faith & Fellowship

For over nine decades, Gloria Dei has built a rich legacy of family faith and deep community care. Dedicated leaders like Louise Palmieri taught Sunday School, organized nursery care, and fostered generations of lifelong friendships through the handicraft guild and community circles, making the South Huntington campus a true sanctuary of warm hospitality.

The Compassion & Outreach Center

Following our collaborative union, the Gloria Dei Campus expanded its midweek focus to become our primary center for community care. The campus is home to **Joseph’s Storehouse**, a client-choice food pantry which served over 15,000 individuals in 2019 alone, and the **Joseph's Storehouse Closet**, which provides clothing and housewares to families in need under the coordination of Kathy Readinger.

Established 1980s

King of Kings Campus Legacy

2611 New York Avenue, Melville, NY 11747

Melville's Community Anchor

Located in Melville, King of Kings has spent decades serving as a vital community anchor and civic hub. Originally structured as an intimate, neighborhood community church, the property was built with a deep commitment to inter-faith cooperation and civic service.

The Temple Beth Torah Sanctuary Partnership

In early 1969, King of Kings played a historic ecumenical role on Long Island. Recognizing that Jewish families in South Huntington needed a home, King of Kings hosted the founding organizing meetings and Friday evening Sabbath worship for **Temple Beth Torah** (Reform Jewish Congregation) while they built their synagogue. The two communities maintained a beautiful Thanksgiving Eve service that rotated between the two buildings for decades.

The Melville Group Recovery Legacy

In 1982, the iconic Saturday Night Melville AA Group was established at King of Kings. The meeting arose from a legendary golf match bet between church member Tony Z. and the pastor. Over time, the meeting grew into a key Melville recovery center, eventually adding Al-Anon and beginner study groups that continue to meet weekly.

Campus Profile

Civic Support: Core polling place for the Suffolk County Board of Elections.

Nesting Partners: Physical sanctuary home for Sung Mood Korean Methodist and Mission Vida Church.

Recovery Support: Weekly host of Melville AA and Al-Anon meetings.
