# **United Lutheran Church of Long Island**

## **Digital Transition Plan & Multi-Campus Strategy**

*Honoring our individual legacies, celebrating our shared seasonal worship, and unifying our digital future.*

## **Part 1: Reframing the Multi-Sanctuary Narrative**

For a consolidated church with three historic campuses, web architecture is not just a technological challenge—it is a pastoral one.1 When legacy members visit the website, they need to see their home sanctuary represented with dignity, pride, and purpose.1  
To minimize anxiety and emphasize unity, the website must avoid portraying any single location as "closed" or "demoted" to a mere administrative building. Instead, we implement a **"Liturgical Seasons & Shared Mission"** design framework 1:

1. **Seasonal Worship Rotations:** The website clearly presents the natural rhythms of the parish.  
   * **The School Year Season (September – June):** Centered in the spacious sanctuary at the **St. Peter's Campus**, hosting unified Sunday morning worship and children's Sunday School.2  
   * **The Summer Season (July – August):** Centered in the intimate, air-conditioned sanctuary at the **Gloria Dei Campus**, offering a cozy, warm summer worship atmosphere.  
   * **The Festival & Holiday Seasons (Advent, Lent, Holy Week, & Thanksgiving):** Alternating liturgies and shared services across **all three sanctuaries** (such as Tenebrae services at King of Kings, Stations of the Cross walks alternating between campuses, and rotating Thanksgiving Eve services).  
2. **Midweek Vitality Spots:** Emphasize that while Sunday worship rotates, each campus remains a hive of unique community service and outreach throughout the week.2 No campus goes dark; instead, each specializes in a vital wing of God’s work.2

## **Part 2: Deep Historical Summaries of Our Legacy Churches**

The unified website must dedicate an immersive "Heritage & History" section to preserve the deep local roots of the Tri-Lutheran Collaborative 5:

### **1\. St. Peter's Evangelical Lutheran Church (Est. 1908\)**

* **Suburban Pioneer Roots:** St. Peter's began doing ministry in 1908 in the area then known as "Fairground". When the Long Island Rail Road built the "Huntington Station" two miles south of the main village, St. Peter's emerged to serve the rapidly expanding railroad suburb.  
* **The "Little White Church":** The congregation initially rented space in a small Presbyterian chapel on New York Avenue before purchasing land on Fairgrounds Avenue to dedicate its first classic wooden church building in 1912\. (This beautiful white structure was eventually sold to the local Greek Orthodox community in the late 1950s to become the founding home of St. Paraskevi Church).  
* **Ogden Court & Modern Vision:** In 1951, the church purchased land on Ogden Court, building its Christian Education Building by 1955\. In 1969, St. Peter's completed its modern, connecting sanctuary, complete with a landmark bell tower, an exceptional pipe organ, and a magnificent stained-glass window depicting Christ as *"The Light of the World"*.  
* **Theological Inclusivity:** Always striving for progress, St. Peter's joined the Association of Evangelical Lutheran Churches (AELC) in 1977, paving the way for the creation of the inclusive Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA), advancing gender equity, and promoting ecumenical collaborations on Long Island.

### **2\. Gloria Dei Lutheran Church (Est. 1931\)**

* **Augustana Heritage:** Founded in 1931, Gloria Dei began as a mission congregation of the historic Augustana Synod, a Swedish branch of American Lutheranism.4 Its Latin name, meaning *"the glory of God,"* was chosen to stand as a constant sign of God's presence in Huntington Station.4  
* **A Community Landmark of Service:** Generations of Huntington families built their faith at Gloria Dei. Members like Louise Palmieri—who taught Sunday School, ran the nursery program, and led the handicraft guild for over 60 years—infused the campus with a warm, family-centered atmosphere.  
* **A Sanctuary of Compassion:** Today, the 18th Street campus serves as the collaborative's Outreach and Compassion center.4 It is the physical home of **Joseph's Storehouse**, a highly active client-choice food pantry that served over 4,000 families and 15,000 individuals in 2019 alone, and the **Joseph's Storehouse Closet** boutique.4 It also sponsors Boy Scout Troop 34 and Cub Scout Pack 406, and hosts weekly recovery groups.4

### **3\. King of Kings Lutheran Church (Melville, NY)**

* **A Melville Community Anchor:** Established as a neighborhood community church, King of Kings has long served as a critical inter-faith, civic, and recovery partner in the Melville area.6  
* **Ecumenical & Jewish Partnership:** In early 1969, King of Kings played a historic role in local Jewish history by hosting the very first organizational and worship meetings for **Temple Beth Torah** (now a prominent Reform Jewish congregation) while they were building their permanent synagogue. To honor this deep relationship, the two congregations held joint, rotating Thanksgiving services for decades.  
* **The Golf Match Miracle:** In 1982, the Melville Group (AA) Saturday night meeting was founded at the church following a legendary friendly golf match wager between church member Tony Z. and the pastor.  
* **A Multicultural & Civic Hub:** Today, the campus continues its civic and multicultural legacy, hosting the **Sung Mood Korean Methodist Church** and **Mission Vida Church**, hosting Melville AAA/Al-Anon groups, and serving as a vital polling place for the Suffolk County Board of Elections.

## **Part 3: Step-by-Step QR Bulletin Setup (Unchanged)**

The permanent dynamic QR workflow remains the most efficient option for a small staff:

1. **Dynamic Page:** Create a page at unitedlutheranli.org/bulletin.  
2. **Pew Signage:** Generate a single QR code pointing to that URL and place it in the pews.  
3. **Weekly Task:** The office assistant simply updates the PDF link behind that URL every Friday. The printed codes never change.
