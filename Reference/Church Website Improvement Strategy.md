# **Strategic Digital Transformation: Web Architecture and User Experience Report for United Lutheran Church of Long Island**

The 2022-2023 consolidation of St. Peter's Evangelical Lutheran Church, Gloria Dei Lutheran Church, and King of Kings Lutheran Church into the unified United Lutheran Church of Long Island represents a significant milestone for mainline Protestant ministry on Long Island.1 Officially organized as the Tri-Lutheran Collaborative, this merger was designed to streamline administrative overhead, leverage physical properties, and pool spiritual and material resources to serve the Huntington and Melville communities.1 However, this physical and administrative convergence has not been mirrored in the congregation’s digital infrastructure.  
Currently, the church's digital presence is restricted to a nested subfolder hosted on the Metropolitan New York Synod (MNYS) website.2 This hosting model poses immediate structural challenges. Search engine algorithms penalize subfolders in favor of standalone top-level domains, severely limiting the church's visibility for local search queries like "family church near Huntington" or "Lutheran services Melville".2 Furthermore, because the church shares server space with the synod, the homepage is cluttered with regional synodical news, such as updates on Brooklyn ministries or synodical policy statements, which distracts from the local identity of the Long Island collaborative.2 Key pages within the directory, such as the "Worship Offerings" page, remain unpopulated with empty "Content here" placeholders, signaling a lack of digital vitality to prospective seekers.6 Transitioning to an independent, high-performance website is essential to establish the newly merged church as a visible and welcoming community hub.2

## **Spatial and Structural Mapping of Campus Functional Identities**

The unique challenge of the United Lutheran Church of Long Island lies in its "converged worship, distributed ministry" configuration.1 While the entire congregation gathers for consolidated Sunday morning worship in the sanctuary at the St. Peter's Campus, the physical assets of the Gloria Dei and King of Kings campuses continue to serve vital localized ministry roles throughout the week.1 A successful web strategy must clearly communicate this physical configuration.2

### **St. Peter's Campus (Worship, Education, and Sunday School Hub)**

Located at 11 Ogden Court, Huntington Station, NY 11746, this site serves as the primary location for Sunday morning worship.1 The campus has a rich history in the local area dating back to 1908, when the surrounding community was known as "Fairground" due to local racetracks.10 After the Long Island Rail Road established its station two miles south of the main village, St. Peter's emerged to serve the rapidly expanding railroad suburb, initially renting space in a small Presbyterian chapel on New York Avenue.10 The congregation grew quickly, dedicating its first white church building on Fairgrounds Avenue in 1912, adding an addition in 1924, and eventually purchasing the Ogden Court property in 1951 to construct the Christian Education Building by 1955\.10 St. Peter's later joined the Association of Evangelical Lutheran Churches in 1977, aligning with the inclusive theological trajectory of the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA) to advance gender equity and ecumenical cooperation.10  
Today, this campus hosts the consolidated Sunday morning worship at 10:00 AM, featuring a weekly Holy Eucharist open to all.1 It is also home to a 9:30 AM children's Sunday School, a post-service coffee and fellowship hour at 11:15 AM, and a monthly Senior Luncheon in the lower auditorium that brings together community members aged 55 and older for hot meals, speakers, and games.2 Midweek programs include a weekly Chancel Choir rehearsal, Bell Choir, and Zoom Yoga sessions.2

### **Gloria Dei Campus (Mercy, Outreach, and Recovery Center)**

Situated at 22 East 18th Street, Huntington Station, NY 11746, this campus acts as the primary compassion and recovery hub of the collaborative.8 Founded in 1931 as a mission of the Swedish Augustana Synod, its Latin name translates to "the glory of God".8 Following the 2022 collaboration, the campus transitioned its Sunday worship to the St. Peter's sanctuary, allowing the 18th Street facility to expand its vital community outreach programs.8  
The campus hosts Joseph's Storehouse, a highly active food pantry affiliated with the Long Island Cares and Island Harvest food banks.8 Operating under a "client choice shopping" model, the pantry distributes fresh meats, fish, local produce, refrigerated goods, and baked items to combat regional food insecurity.8 In 2019, the pantry successfully served over 4,000 families and 15,000 individuals.8 To further address material needs, the campus opened Joseph's Storehouse Closet in January 2023, offering a weekly selection of clothing, shoes, and housewares under the coordination of Kathy Readinger.8 Gloria Dei also sponsors Boy Scout Troop 34 and Cub Scout Pack 406, hosting their community service initiatives alongside weekly Narcotics Anonymous recovery meetings and in-person Thursday Bible studies.2

### **King of Kings Campus (Civic Partnership and Inter-Church Hub)**

Located at 2611 New York Avenue, Melville, NY 11747, this campus is positioned as a critical civic partnership and inter-church center.7 Established as a neighborhood community church, its integration into the collaborative has enabled the building to continue hosting local partners.7 It currently acts as the physical home for the Sung Mood Korean Methodist Church and the Mission Vida Church, ensuring the property remains a multi-cultural site of Christian worship.7 Furthermore, the building serves as a venue for local Melville AAA meetings and is utilized as a polling location by the Suffolk County Board of Elections during primary and general elections.7

## **Modern Church Website Usability and Engagement Standards**

The landscape of religious non-profit web design has shifted from static, bulletin-board layouts to dynamic, engagement-driven experiences.4 High-performing church websites prioritize mobile-first architectures, with data showing that over 75% of users browse church websites from mobile devices.5 This trend necessitates a mobile-first design, featuring sticky bottom navigation menus, easily clickable button targets, and clean transitions.4 Furthermore, websites must load in under two seconds to prevent user drop-off and support search visibility, as Google’s ranking algorithms penalize slow-loading sites.5  
Beyond technical performance, effective church web design balances the needs of two distinct groups: prospective visitors searching for a spiritual home, and active members seeking event information.4 For visitors, the homepage must establish a clear first impression within five seconds.15 It must answer core questions: what the church believes, when and where it meets, and what to expect.4 Leading congregations achieve this through a "Plan Your Visit" landing page, which outlines parking, children’s registration, dress codes, and details like the availability of coffee.16 Rather than using high-concept abstract videos, effective sites use authentic welcome videos and photography of real people—such as choir practices, active children's classrooms, or community food distribution—to build trust.4  
A direct theological connection exists between web design and the Lutheran focus on welcome and grace.11 A church that proclaims "all are welcome" must reflect that commitment through digital inclusion and strict compliance with Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG).4 This includes high color-contrast ratios, keyboard and voice navigation support, screen-reader compatible alternative text on all images, and transcripts or captions for video sermons.4  
At the denominational level, the redesign of the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA) website in March 2026 offers an important blueprint for local congregations.20 The new ELCA.org represents a paradigm shift away from complex theological jargon toward simple, approachable language designed for the "faith-curious".20 Central to this user experience is a guided exploration tool that answers visitor questions dynamically by pulling resources from across the church's digital footprint.20 This highlights a key lesson for the United Lutheran Church: the website should speak the language of everyday seekers looking for community, justice, and connection, rather than utilizing insider administrative terminology.20

## **Actionable Migration and Implementation Strategy**

To execute this digital upgrade successfully, the leadership of United Lutheran Church of Long Island should adopt a phased development and deployment roadmap.2

### **Stage One: Governance, Hosting Setup, and Domain Security**

The first step requires the church council to appoint a web committee comprised of representatives from all three campus histories.1 This committee should immediately purchase a top-level domain, such as unitedlutheranli.org, to secure an independent web identity and improve local search visibility.2 Additionally, the committee must select the underlying hosting platform.28 For a congregation seeking a balance of modern design and easy volunteer updates, a premium Squarespace 7.1 template (such as *Cove* or *Community Church*) or a dedicated Tithe.ly Site is recommended to minimize technical overhead.16

### **Stage Two: Spatial and Content Audit**

Before drafting page layouts, the web committee should conduct a complete audit of current ministries.4 This step must map out how to present the church's multi-campus model online.9 Copy should be written to clarify that Sunday morning worship is consolidated at St. Peter's 1, while Gloria Dei serves as the food pantry and community outreach hub 8, and King of Kings serves as the Melville civic and inter-church partnership facility.7 Presenting the distinct programs of each location prevents confusion and ensures visitors arrive at the correct campus.2

### **Stage Three: Media Creation and Visual Storytelling**

The committee should coordinate a media collection day.4 A volunteer or hired photographer should capture high-quality images and video clips of real ministry moments.4 This includes shots of the choir rehearsals, the monthly Senior Luncheon, children's Sunday School, and the operations of Joseph's Storehouse and Closet.2 These visual assets will replace generic stock graphics, providing an authentic, welcoming feel on the homepage.4

### **Stage Four: Integration of Functional Plugins**

To turn the website from a static brochure into an active community tool, several key integrations must be established.23

* **Secure Online Giving**: Embed a responsive tithing portal (such as Tithe.ly or Donorbox) to allow simple, secure credit card and digital wallet donations directly on the giving page.5  
* **Sermon Media Library**: Configure a searchable sermon page that allows visitors to filter past audio and video messages by series, scripture topic, or speaker.16  
* **Shared Ministry Calendar**: Integrate a unified calendar (using Planning Center Online or Google Calendar) to sync upcoming educational programs, Zoom yoga, Thursday Bible studies, NA meetings, and Joseph's Storehouse operating hours across all three campuses.2

### **Stage Five: Search Engine Optimization and Launch Protocols**

Before launching the site, the committee must configure local SEO and accessibility settings.4 This includes adding schema markup for events, pastors, and service times, which helps search engines and voice assistants pull accurate local data.5 Descriptive alternative text must be added to all images, and font-scaling and color-contrast ratios should be verified against WCAG standards.4 Finally, the committee should claim the church's Google Business Profile, input the correct campus details, and coordinate with the Metropolitan New York Synod to redirect the old subfolder URL to the new independent domain.2 This preserves search authority and seamlessly routes visitors to the updated, professional web home.2
